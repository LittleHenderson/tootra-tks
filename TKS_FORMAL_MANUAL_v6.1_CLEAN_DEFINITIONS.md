## Symbol Conventions (Elements vs Foundations vs Acquisitions)

This manual uses three distinct symbol namespaces. These must never be conflated.

### Elements (40 Elements)

- **Symbols:** A1–A10, B1–B10, C1–C10, D1–D10
- **Pattern:** Capital letter + digits (e.g., A2, C7, D10)
- **Role:** World-level modes representing the 10 Noetics manifesting through each of the four Worlds (Spiritual/Mental/Emotional/Physical).

### Foundations (28 Sub-Foundations)

- **Symbols:** 1a–7d
- **Pattern:** Digit + lowercase letter (e.g., 3b, 5d, 7a)
- **Role:** Life-domain structures representing the 7 Foundations × 4 World aspects.

### Acquisitions (D/W/P, A22)

- **Symbols:** D1–D7, W1–W7, P1–P7, A22
- **Pattern:** Capital letter + digit (e.g., D3, W5, P6) or A22
- **Role:** Desire/Wisdom/Power acquisitions tied to each Foundation, plus the global Meta-Desire A22.

### Noetics (Operators)

- **Symbols:** ^0–^9
- **Pattern:** Caret + digit (e.g., ^1, ^7)
- **Role:** Operators/aspects of consciousness that modify or tag other TKS values. Noetics are not standalone quantities.

**Throughout this manual:**
- "Element" refers only to A1–D10
- "Foundation" refers only to 1a–7d
- "Acquisition" refers only to D1–D7, W1–W7, P1–P7, A22
- "Noetic" refers only to ^0–^9
- "TKS value" refers to any Element, Foundation, or composite state that can participate in arithmetic

# 1. The Ten Noetics (Formal Definitions)

The **Noetics** are the ten fundamental operations or aspects of consciousness as Mind and Idea interact. They are indexed 0-9, with ^0 (Idea) serving as the neutral baseline.

## 1.1 Canonical Index

| Index | Symbol | Name | Short Label |
|-------|--------|------|-------------|
| 0 | ^0 | Idea | Content/Thing |
| 1 | ^1 | Mind | Processor/Awareness |
| 2 | ^2 | Positive | Attraction/Affinity |
| 3 | ^3 | Negative | Aversion/Repulsion |
| 4 | ^4 | Vibration | Resonance/Impression |
| 5 | ^5 | Female | Receptivity/Composition (of Mind) |
| 6 | ^6 | Male | Structure/Composition (of Idea) |
| 7 | ^7 | Rhythm | Timing/Repetition |
| 8 | ^8 | Above/Cause | Trigger/Stimulus |
| 9 | ^9 | Below/Effect | Response/Result |

---

## 1.2 Formal Definitions

### Noetic 0 - Idea (^0)

**Definition:** The content itself; any "thing" inside or outside the Mind. All possible somethings.

**Clarification:** ^0 is the neutral baseline - the Idea as it is, before any particular mental relation is applied. This includes thoughts, objects, concepts, feelings, sensations, or any identifiable "thing" that can be processed by Mind.

**Functional Role:** The universal content that consciousness processes. The "data" in the Mind-as-processor model.

---

### Noetic 1 - Mind (^1)

**Definition:** The various degrees of awareness that process, store, output, and take in Ideas.

**Clarification:** This is the aware processor. When ^1 is active, the Mind is actually noticing and working with an Idea. Mind is not a static container but a dynamic processor with varying degrees of engagement.

**Functional Role:** The processor that operates on Ideas. Directed attention; consciousness processing.

---

### Noetic 2 - Positive (^2)

**Definition:** An Idea the Mind has affinity for and is attracted toward.

**Clarification:** This is the "yes" or "toward" relation. The Mind moves closer to this Idea, gives it energy and attention. Positive does not mean "good" in a moral sense; it means attractive to that particular Mind.

**Functional Role:** Attraction, desire, pull toward. The "want" relation between Mind and Idea.

---

### Noetic 3 - Negative (^3)

**Definition:** An Idea the Mind is averse to and repelled by.

**Clarification:** This is the "no" or "away" relation. The Mind withdraws from this Idea, pushes it out, or resists it. Negative does not mean "bad" in a moral sense; it means aversive to that particular Mind.

**Functional Role:** Aversion, rejection, push away. The "don't want" relation between Mind and Idea.

---

### Noetic 4 - Vibration (^4)

**Definition:** The degree to which an Idea resonates with the Mind; the Idea's vibratory impression on Mind.

**Clarification:** ^4 describes how strongly and in what pattern an Idea "rings" in the Mind when present. High vibration means the Idea makes a deep, lasting impression. Low vibration means weak or fleeting registration.

**Functional Role:** Resonance, impression strength, frequency of impact. How deeply an Idea "lands" in consciousness.

---

### Noetic 5 - Female (^5)

**Definition:** A Mind impregnated with an Idea; the Ideas a Mind is composed of.

**Clarification:** This is the receptive aspect - Mind as a womb holding and organizing the Ideas that live inside it. Female (^5) represents the conditioning, beliefs, memories, and patterns that constitute a Mind's current composition.

**Functional Role:** Receptivity, internal composition of Mind. What the Mind already contains and how it is structured to receive new Ideas.

---

### Noetic 6 - Male (^6)

**Definition:** An Idea impregnated with another Idea; the Ideas an Idea is composed of.

**Clarification:** This is the structural/compositional aspect - one Idea containing or shaping others inside itself. Male (^6) represents the internal structure and sub-components of any Idea.

**Functional Role:** Structure, content, internal composition of Ideas. The "what's inside" of any Idea.

---

### Noetic 7 - Rhythm (^7)

**Definition:** The rate and pattern by which a Mind is exposed to an Idea, entraining and conditioning it.

**Clarification:** Rhythm is exposure over time - repeated contact in a pattern that trains the Mind into a habit or groove. Regular exposure creates entrainment; irregular exposure creates instability.

**Functional Role:** Timing, repetition, cycles. How often and in what pattern Mind encounters Idea.

---

### Noetic 8 - Above/Cause (^8)

**Definition:** An Idea that triggers a different Idea than itself to come to Mind.

**Clarification:** ^8 is causal - it's the trigger or "above" Idea that calls something else into awareness. The Above/Cause Idea is the stimulus; what it triggers is the Below/Effect.

**Functional Role:** Trigger, cause, stimulus. The initiating Idea in a cause-effect chain.

---

### Noetic 9 - Below/Effect (^9)

**Definition:** The Idea brought to Mind by an Above/Cause Idea or trigger.

**Clarification:** ^9 is the effect - the "below" Idea that appears because ^8 was present first. This is the response that follows the stimulus.

**Functional Role:** Result, effect, response. The resulting Idea in a cause-effect chain.

---

## 1.3 Noetic Interaction Summary

The Noetics form a complete system for describing how consciousness operates:

- **^0 (Idea)** - What is being processed
- **^1 (Mind)** - What does the processing
- **^2/^3 (Positive/Negative)** - The valence of the Mind-Idea relationship

- **^6 (Male)** - The current structure of Idea (being received)

# 2. Tootra Arithmetic - The Four Operations

The four arithmetic operations describe how Ideas combine, separate, amplify, and diminish in consciousness.

## 2.1 Operation Table

| Operation | Symbol | Core Meaning | Use For |
|-----------|--------|--------------|---------|
| Addition | + | Combining, joining, associating | Bringing Ideas together, forming bonds or composites |
| Subtraction | - | Removing, separating, eliminating | Releasing, cutting away, removing an Idea or influence |
| Multiplication | x | Amplifying, increasing, expanding | Intensifying an Idea, scaling up its influence or effect |
| Division | / | Diminishing, apportioning, resolving | Reducing power, distributing, or completing a cycle |

---

## 2.2 Formal Definitions

### Addition (+)

**Definition:** Combining two or more Ideas so that all are present together in the resulting state.

**Operational Meaning:** When A + B is performed, both A and B become simultaneously active or associated in the Mind. The result contains both Ideas in relationship.

**Examples:**
- Health + Confidence = A state where both health and confidence are actively present
- Knowledge + Action = Knowing combined with doing (applied knowledge)
- Goal + Plan = A desired outcome combined with a structured approach

**Use Cases:** Creating composites, establishing associations, joining previously separate elements, building complex states from simpler components.

---

### Subtraction (-)

**Definition:** Removing or separating one Idea's influence from another.

**Operational Meaning:** When A - B is performed, B's influence is removed from, or separated from, A. The result is A without B's presence or effect.

**Examples:**
- Health - Fear = Health with fear removed (unobstructed vitality)
- Confidence - Doubt = Confidence with doubt eliminated
- Goal - Obstacle = The goal with the blocking factor removed

**Use Cases:** Releasing attachments, removing blocks, eliminating negative influences, separating ideas that were previously joined.

---

### Multiplication (x)

**Definition:** Amplifying an Idea by another factor, intensifying its presence or effect.

**Operational Meaning:** When A x B is performed, A is strengthened, expanded, or intensified by the factor of B. This is not simple addition but exponential or multiplicative increase.

**Examples:**
- Desire x Focus = Desire amplified by concentrated attention
- Skill x Practice = Skill multiplied through repeated application
- Intention x Emotion = Intention charged with emotional intensity

**Use Cases:** Intensifying effects, scaling up influence, reinforcing through repetition or charging, expanding the power of an existing Idea.

---

### Division (/)

**Definition:** Reducing, distributing, or resolving an Idea's concentrated power.

**Operational Meaning:** When A / B is performed, A's concentrated power is divided, distributed, or resolved by factor B. This can mean completion of a cycle, reduction of intensity, or distribution across multiple targets.

**Examples:**
- Obsession / Release = Obsessive focus divided by releasing action (resulting in reduced fixation)
- Energy / Tasks = Available energy distributed across multiple tasks
- Cycle / Completion = A pattern resolved through reaching its natural end

**Use Cases:** Reducing excessive intensity, completing cycles, distributing concentrated energy, resolving or closing out processes.

---

## 2.3 Arithmetic Interaction with TKS Values

The operations can be applied to any TKS value (Elements, Foundations, or composite states). For example:

- **A2 + C2** = Spiritual attraction (Element A2) joined with emotional attraction (Element C2)—two Elements in different Worlds considered together.
- **5b − 3b** = Mental composition (Foundation 5b) with mental aversion (Foundation 3b) removed—a Foundation-level subtraction in the mental aspect.
- **Desire × Rhythm** = Desire amplified through repeated exposure—a state (Desire) intensified by rhythmic application.
- **Vibration ÷ Time** = Resonance distributed/diminished over time—a vibratory pattern reduced or spread out across temporal exposure.

---

## 2.4 Invalid Mixed Forms (Noetic + Foundation)

**Noetics (^0…^9) are operators, not standalone quantities.**

Expressions of the form "Noetic + Foundation" (e.g., `^1 + 3d`) are **not defined** in this formal system because Noetics are not values that can be added—they are transformation operators that modify values.

### Correct Usage Pattern

To combine a Noetic aspect with a Foundation, first apply the Noetic operator to the Foundation, then perform arithmetic on the resulting tagged values:

| Invalid | Correct |
|---------|---------|
| `^1 + 3d` | `3d^1` (Foundation 3d viewed through Noetic ^1) |
| `^2 - 5b` | `5b^2` then arithmetic on tagged result |
| `Noetic + Element` | `Element^Noetic` or `(Element^Noetic) + (Element^Noetic)` |

### Rule

**Noetics modify; they do not add.** Always apply the Noetic superscript first, producing a tagged Foundation or Element, then combine tagged values using standard Tootra arithmetic operations.

**Definition:** The highest level of reality; the domain of archetypes, divine principles, and ultimate meaning.

**Definition:** The level of intellect, thought, belief, and conceptualization.

**Characteristics:**
- Domain of ideas as cognitive constructs
- Beliefs, frameworks, mental models

---

**Definition:** The level of feeling, emotion, intuition, and formative patterns.

**Characteristics:**
- Domain of emotional experience
- Where mental concepts acquire feeling-tone
- Intuition and gut responses
- Shapes how thoughts become motivating desires

---

### Assiah (D) - Physical World

**Definition:** The level of matter, body, action, and concrete manifestation.

**Characteristics:**
- Domain of physical reality
- Where all higher-world patterns finally manifest
- Actions, objects, environments, bodies
- The "densest" and most concrete level

---

## 3.3 Cross-World Dynamics

**Downward Flow (A -> B -> C -> D):**
Spiritual principles (A) inform mental frameworks (B), which shape emotional patterns (C), which manifest as physical behaviors and realities (D).

**Upward Flow (D -> C -> B -> A):**
Physical changes (D) can shift emotional states (C), which alter thought patterns (B), which can ultimately transform spiritual orientation (A).

**Principle:** Work at any level affects all others, but changes at higher levels (closer to A) create more fundamental and lasting transformations.

## 5.3 Distinguishing Similar Sub-Foundations

Several sub-foundations may appear similar at first glance. The following clarifications establish non-overlapping boundaries:

### 1b (Mental Association) vs. 2b (Mental Wisdom)

- **1b** governs *how* thoughts connect to each other (the linking mechanism)
- **2b** governs *what* is known (the content of knowledge)
- *Example:* 1b determines that "fire" links to "heat" in your mind; 2b determines whether you understand combustion chemistry.

### 3c (Emotional Vitality) vs. 4c (Emotional Companionship)

- **3c** governs the *energy* available for emotional life (resilience, aliveness)
- **4c** governs the *bonds* formed with others (attachment, intimacy)
- *Example:* You can have high 3c (emotional energy) but low 4c (few close bonds), or vice versa.

### 5b (Mental Power) vs. 2b (Mental Wisdom)

- **2b** governs *knowing* something (having the knowledge)
- **5b** governs *influencing* through that knowledge (persuading, strategizing)
- *Example:* 2b is understanding economics; 5b is convincing others to adopt your economic policy.

### 6d (Physical Structure) vs. 5d (Physical Power)

- **6d** governs *organized resources* (wealth, systems, material order)
- **5d** governs *capacity to cause physical change* (strength, control, making things happen)
- *Example:* 6d is having money in the bank; 5d is the ability to use that money effectively to achieve outcomes.

### 7a (Spiritual Rhythm) vs. 1a (Spiritual Association)

- **1a** governs *connections* between spiritual meanings (synchronicity, resonance)
- **7a** governs *timing* of spiritual unfolding (cycles, stages, cosmic rhythm)
- *Example:* 1a perceives that two events are meaningfully linked; 7a senses that now is the right time for spiritual action.

## 5.4 Foundation Integration Principles

### 5.4.1 Vertical Integration (Same Foundation, All Worlds)

Within a single Foundation, the four world-levels form a causal chain flowing from abstract to concrete:

**A (Spiritual) → B (Mental) → C (Emotional) → D (Physical)**

Spiritual principles inform mental frameworks, which shape emotional patterns, which manifest as physical behaviors and realities.

**Implication:** Blocks in higher worlds cascade down. If 5a (spiritual power) is weak, 5d (physical power) will feel hollow or unsustainable regardless of external resources. Address higher-world blocks first for lasting change.

**Example:** Someone trying to build physical wealth (6d) while lacking a coherent worldview (6a) or mental organization (6b) will struggle to maintain gains.

### 5.4.2 Horizontal Integration (Same World, All Foundations)

Within a single World, all 7 Foundations interact as an interdependent system:

**1x → 2x → 3x → 4x → 5x → 6x → 7x** (where x = a, b, c, or d)

**Example (B-World / Mental):**
- 1b (associations) determine what 2b (knowledge) you can access
- 2b requires 3b (mental energy) to acquire and use

# 7. The Recursive Prerequisite Model (RPM) - Formal Structure

The **Recursive Prerequisite Model (RPM)** is the TKS diagnostic algorithm for identifying exactly what is blocking manifestation and precisely what needs to be addressed.

## 7.1 Core Concept

**RPM Principle:** For any goal, if any of D/W/P is missing or significantly underdeveloped, manifestation will either fail or collapse under stress. The RPM systematically identifies the first unmet prerequisite and traces it to an actionable root.

### How RPM Uses Acquisitions

RPM operates on the 22 Acquisitions defined in Section 6:

- For each **Foundation F(n)**, the required Acquisitions are the D/W/P triad: **{D(n), W(n), P(n)}**
- Example: For F5 (Power), the required set is {D5, W5, P5} = engine IDs {13, 14, 15}
- The **12 Prerequisite States** (Kings/Queens/Jacks) describe the *developmental level* of each Acquisition

RPM checks whether each D/W/P Acquisition is present and sufficiently developed, then recursively decomposes any blocks until reaching actionable steps.

---

## 7.2 Formal Definition

### Sets

- **F** = {F1, F2, ..., F7} - The set of 7 Foundations (or their 28 sub-foundations)
- **A** = {D1, W1, P1, D2, W2, P2, ..., D7, W7, P7, A22} - The set of 22 Acquisitions
- **SD** = {sd1, sd2, ...} - The set of Sub-Desires generated during decomposition

### Functions

- **triad: F -> P(A)** - Maps each Foundation to its D/W/P triad: triad(Fn) = {Dn, Wn, Pn}
- **s: A -> [0, 1]** - The Status function; returns the developmental level of an Acquisition (0 = absent, 1 = fully present)
- **m: A -> P(A)** - The Decomposition function; maps an unmet Acquisition to its prerequisite Acquisitions
- **threshold: A -> [0, 1]** - The minimum level required for manifestation (typically ~0.75 for stable manifestation)

---

## 7.3 The RPM Algorithm

```
ALGORITHM: RPM_Diagnose(goal in F)

INPUT: goal (Prime Desire)

1. required_acquisitions <- f(goal)

2. FOR EACH acquisition a IN required_acquisitions:

3.     IF s(a) = 0 THEN                       // Acquisition unmet
4.         sub_acquisitions <- m(a)            // Decompose
5.         IF sub_acquisitions = empty THEN
6.             RETURN a                        // Base case: directly actionable
7.         ELSE
8.             FOR EACH sub_a IN sub_acquisitions:
9.                 IF s(sub_a) = 0 THEN
10.                    RETURN RPM_Diagnose(sub_a)  // Recurse
11.                END IF
12.            END FOR
13.        END IF
14.    END IF

15. END FOR

16. RETURN NULL                               // All prerequisites met
```

---

## 7.4 Properties of the RPM System

### Termination Guarantee
The recursion terminates because the decomposition function m eventually reaches atomic Acquisitions (where m(a) = empty). These correspond to states achievable through direct Noetic action.

### Determinism
Given identical input states (same goal, same s() values), the algorithm produces identical output. No mystical ambiguity.

### Completeness
Every blocked goal can be traced to specific unmet base-level Acquisitions. Nothing is "inexplicable."

### Soundness
If RPM_Diagnose returns NULL, then by definition all prerequisites are satisfied and the goal should manifest (barring external interference or misdiagnosis).

---

## 7.5 The RPM Process (Practical Steps)

### Step 1: Define Prime Desire
What do you actually want? Be specific. Identify the relevant Foundation(s).

### Step 2: Scan Prerequisites
Starting with Desire (Kings), assess your current state for the Prime Desire against the 12 Prerequisites. Which state accurately describes your current level of Desire, Power, and Wisdom for this specific goal?

### Step 3: Identify the Block
Is any Prerequisite state insufficient for manifestation? The first insufficient Prerequisite you hit in the D -> W -> P sequence is your Block.

### Step 4: Formulate Sub-Desire
The Block automatically becomes your new goal - the Sub-Desire to acquire the necessary Prerequisite state.

### Step 5: Recurse
Apply the RPM scan (Steps 2-4) to this Sub-Desire. Keep drilling down until you hit a Prerequisite Block that can be directly addressed through Noetic action.

### Step 6: Execute Noetic Action
Perform the necessary actions (guided by Mind and informed by Wisdom) to shift the state of the base-level blocked Prerequisite.

### Step 7: Ascend & Re-Scan
Once a Sub-Desire is fulfilled, move back up the Desire Tree to the previous level and re-scan the Prerequisites from that point. Repeat until the Prime Desire is achieved.

# 9. Noetic Fractals – Structured Noetic Sequences

Noetic fractals are structured sequences of Noetic actions applied to a target Idea, Element, or Foundation. They are the "programs" or protocols of TKS: reusable patterns that describe **how** Mind repeatedly relates to an Idea over time.

A single Noetic (`^0…^9`) describes one kind of Mind–Idea relation (awareness, Positive, Negative, Vibration, Female/Male, Rhythm, Cause/Effect). A **Noetic fractal** describes a **pattern** of these relations applied in sequence and, potentially, in repetition.

---

## 9.1 Definition and Notation

A **Noetic fractal pattern** is a finite, ordered sequence of Noetic indices:

- Let F = (n₁, n₂, …, nₖ) where each nᵢ ∈ {0,1,…,9}.

We write this pattern as:

- Unicode form: **⟨n₁:n₂:…:nₖ⟩**
- ASCII form: **`<<n1:n2:...:nk>>`**

These two notations are equivalent. The Unicode form ⟨…⟩ is the canonical mathematical notation; the ASCII form `<<…>>` is used where Unicode is inconvenient (plain-text, code).

Examples:

- ⟨1:4:7⟩ – awareness → vibration → rhythm
- ⟨8:1:4⟩ – cause → awareness → vibration
- ⟨2:3⟩ – positive → negative (dual polarity pattern)

A **Noetic fractal operator** is the function induced by such a pattern when applied to a TKS value (Idea, Element, Foundation, or composite).

---

## 9.2 Applying a Fractal to a Value

Let X be any TKS value that can accept Noetic application (for example: an Element like A2, a Foundation like 3d, or a composite state).

Given a fractal pattern F = ⟨n₁ : n₂ : … : nₖ⟩, the **single-pass application** of F to X is defined as:

```
F(X) = ⟨n₁ : n₂ : … : nₖ⟩(X) := (((X^n₁)^n₂) …^nₖ)
```

That is:

1. Start with X.
2. Apply Noetic n₁ to get X^n₁.
3. Apply Noetic n₂ to that result, and so on, until nₖ.

**Order is left-to-right**: `⟨1:4:7⟩(3d)` means:

1. Apply ^1 (Mind/Awareness) to 3d
2. Then apply ^4 (Vibration) to that result
3. Then apply ^7 (Rhythm) to that result

Special cases:

- If k = 1, ⟨n₁⟩(X) = X^n₁ (a single Noetic application).
- If k = 0 (empty pattern), we treat F as the identity operator: it leaves X unchanged.

---

## 9.3 Composition of Fractals

Two fractals can be composed by **concatenating** their Noetic sequences.

Let:

- F = ⟨a₁ : … : aₘ⟩
- G = ⟨b₁ : … : bₙ⟩

Then the composite fractal G ∘ F (apply F, then G) is:

```
(G ∘ F)(X) = G(F(X)) = ⟨a₁ : … : aₘ : b₁ : … : bₙ⟩(X)
```

Thus:

- Composition = sequence concatenation left-to-right.
- There is an identity fractal: the empty pattern ⟨⟩, which leaves any X unchanged.
- A single Noetic `⟨n⟩` is a fractal of length 1.

In practice, fractals may be **simplified** using Noetic identities (e.g., identity Noetic, dual cancellations) where defined elsewhere in the TKS composition rules. This manual does not introduce a full 10×10 composition table here; it only defines the sequencing behavior.

## 9.4 Iteration and Dynamic Behavior

A key use of Noetic fractals is to describe **repeated** patterns of Mind–Idea interaction over time.

Given a fractal pattern F and initial value X, define:

- F⁽¹⁾(X) = F(X)
- F⁽²⁾(X) = F(F(X))
- F⁽ᵐ⁾(X) = F(F⁽ᵐ⁻¹⁾(X)) for m ≥ 2

We are often interested in the **long-term behavior** of the sequence:

```
X, F⁽¹⁾(X), F⁽²⁾(X), F⁽³⁾(X), …
```

Based on this behavior, we classify fractals (relative to a class of inputs) into **behavioral types**:

- **Stabilizing fractal**
  - Repeated application of F drives the state toward a stable configuration.
  - After enough iterations, the system approaches a fixed point or a small, bounded region of states.
  - Intuitively: "this pattern tends to settle the system."

- **Amplifying fractal**
  - Repeated application of F tends to increase activation or intensity in a domain (e.g., foundation activations, emotional amplitude), often until some practical limit or saturation is reached.
  - Intuitively: "this pattern tends to build and intensify."

- **Oscillatory fractal**
  - Repeated application of F cycles the state among a finite set of configurations (periodic behavior) without settling to a single fixed point.
  - Intuitively: "this pattern tends to alternate or flip between modes."

These classifications are **behavioral**: the same abstract pattern may be stabilizing for one target (e.g., 3d health in a calm context) and amplifying for another (e.g., 5d power in a volatile context). In practice, TKS practitioners empirically test and catalogue which fractals behave in which way for particular Foundations and goals.

## 9.5 Examples of Noetic Fractals in TKS Practice

Some fractal patterns occur frequently in TKS work. A few illustrative examples:

- **⟨1:4:7⟩ – Awareness → Vibration → Rhythm**
  - Often used as a **harmonizing/stabilizing** pattern.
  - 1 (Mind/Awareness): bring the Idea or Foundation into clear consciousness.
  - 4 (Vibration): energize the relation, give it resonance.
  - 7 (Rhythm): establish repeated exposure or practice, turning it into a habit or pattern in time.

- **⟨8:1:4⟩ – Cause → Awareness → Vibration**
  - A **cause-oriented** pattern:
    - 8 (Above/Cause): identify the initiating Idea or trigger.
    - 1 (Mind/Awareness): make it conscious.
    - 4 (Vibration): amplify the resonance of the chosen cause.

- **⟨5:6:9⟩ – Female → Male → Effect**
  - A **polarity and manifestation** pattern:
    - 5 (Female): receptive field, Ideals the Mind is composed of.
    - 6 (Male): projecting Idea structure into form.
    - 9 (Below/Effect): the concrete Idea or manifestation that results.

These are examples, not an exhaustive catalogue. In practice, entire libraries of fractals are constructed for specific Foundations (health, relationships, power, material flow, continuation) and for specific ritual or meditative protocols.

## 9.6 Relationship to Noetics, Arithmetic, and RPM

Noetic fractals sit **on top of** the basic structures defined in earlier sections:

- **Noetics (Section 1):**
  - Define *what kind* of Mind–Idea relation each index (0–9) represents.

- **Tootra Arithmetic (Section 2):**
  - Defines how multiple Ideas or states combine, separate, amplify, or resolve (`+ − × ÷`).

- **Foundations and Acquisitions (Sections 5–6):**
  - Define *what* is being transformed (life domains, Desire/Wisdom/Power states, meta-Desire A22).

- **RPM (Section 7):**
  - Decides **when** a transformation is allowed, by checking whether prerequisite Acquisitions are present and mature enough.

Within that framework:

- A **fractal pattern** (⟨…⟩) specifies **how** Noetics are applied to a chosen target (Element, Foundation, or state) over time.
- RPM determines whether the prerequisites (Desire/Wisdom/Power) are in place for that fractal to be effective.
- Tootra arithmetic describes how results of multiple fractal applications can be combined or disentangled.

Thus, a TKS protocol for manifestation typically has the structure:

1. Choose the target Foundation / sub-foundation (e.g., 3d physical health).
2. Diagnose D/W/P acquisitions and States using RPM.
3. Select an appropriate Noetic fractal (or sequence of fractals).
4. Apply the fractal over time (meditation, practice, exposure, action).
5. Use Tootra arithmetic and RPM to interpret and adjust the evolving state.

This section defines the formal behavior of the fractals themselves; catalogues of specific, named fractals for practical work belong in separate application manuals or curricula built on top of this core formalism.

# Appendix A – Summary of Resolved Decisions

This appendix summarizes key doctrinal and formal decisions that were previously flagged as ambiguous in early drafts of TKS v6.1 and have now been resolved in this Clean Definitions edition.

---

## A.1 Cross-Type Tootra Arithmetic

**Issue:** How should operations like "Noetic + Foundation" be treated?

**Decision:**
Noetics (`^0…^9`) are operators, not standalone quantities. Expressions of the form `^1 + 3d` or "Noetic + Foundation" are not defined. The correct pattern is:

- Apply the Noetic to a value: `3d^1`, `A2^4`, etc.
- Then use arithmetic (`+ − × ÷`) on the resulting Ideas/states.

This is formalized in Section 2.4 ("Invalid Mixed Forms (Noetic + Foundation)").

---

## A.2 Foundation 7 Naming

**Issue:** Different drafts referred to Foundation 7 as "Lust", "Sex/Reproduction", "Rhythm of Purpose", etc.

**Decision:**
The canonical short name for Foundation 7 is:

- **Foundation 7 – Continuation / Reproduction**

Its four sub-foundations are:

- 7a – Spiritual Continuation
- 7b – Mental Continuation
- 7c – Emotional Lust/Bonding
- 7d – Physical Sex/Reproduction

See Section 5 for the full table and definitions.

---

## A.3 Acquisition 22 (A22)

**Issue:** The role of Acquisition 22 ("Desire for desire") was unclear: is it tied to one Foundation, and does it have W22/P22 companions?

**Decision:**

- **Symbolic ID:** A22
- **Category:** Meta-Desire (D\*)
- **Scope:** Global / cross-foundational
- **Function:** A22 is the activation of the desire to desire itself—the rekindling, intensification, or redirection of Desire across existing D/W/P channels. It is the engine of wanting itself.
- **Tree position:** Geometrically represented as the polarity loop between Noetic 6 (Male) and Noetic 5 (Female), connecting structured pattern to receptive field.
- **No W22/P22:** There is no W22 or P22; A22 stands alone as a unique meta-Desire.

See Section 6.3 and the subsection "A22: The Meta-Desire (Special Case)" for details.

---

## A.4 States vs Acquisitions

**Issue:** Are the 12 Prerequisite States (Kings/Queens/Jacks) separate Acquisitions, or how do they relate to D/W/P?

**Decision:**

- The 12 Prerequisite States are **qualitative overlays**, not new Acquisitions.
- They describe developmental maturity levels (e.g., King = mature/strong, Queen = functional/operational, Jack = learning/immature).
- They can be applied as evaluation lenses to any Acquisition (D/W/P) in any Foundation.

In the RPM formalism, the status function `s(a)` considers both:

- whether the Acquisition is present, and
- whether its State has reached sufficiency:

  - `s(a) = 1` only when the associated State is mature enough (King/Queen level)
  - `s(a) = 0` when the Acquisition is still at a Jack/immature level.

See Section 6.5 ("The 12 Prerequisite States") for full details.

---

## A.5 Tree of Life as Multigraph

**Issue:** Some paths in the Acquisition Tree appear to connect the same Noetics more than once, suggesting "duplicate" edges.

**Decision:**

- The TKS Tree of Life is formally a **multigraph**: the same pair of Noetic nodes can have multiple edges.
- Each edge represents a distinct Acquisition with its own name, semantic role, and D/W/P classification.
- Apparent duplicates are not errors; they reflect multiple distinct flows of consciousness sharing the same geometric route.

See Section 6.3 and the Tree-of-Life discussion for more.
